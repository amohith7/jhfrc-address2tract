Address to Census Tract

Upload a spreadsheet of addresses and get back the U.S. Census tract for each one. No software to install.

Your file stays on your computer. It is opened and processed inside this browser tab and is never uploaded to or stored on this site. Only the address text of each record is sent to the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder (and, for addresses the Census cannot find, to the OpenStreetMap Nominatim geocoder) to look up its location.

Questions

What do the match statuses mean?
Matched
Found by the Census geocoder in its current address file.
Matched_Fallback
Found by the Census geocoder in its 2020 address file, or after tidying the street abbreviation.
Matched_External
Located by the OpenStreetMap Nominatim geocoder, then assigned to its Census tract.
No_Match
No geocoder could place the address. Check it for typos or a missing street number. PO boxes are never matched because they have no physical location.
Rejected
The row was missing its ID or its address.
Which tract boundaries are used?

The Census Bureau's current tract boundaries, as returned by the Census geocoder. Tract codes are 11 digits: state (2), county (3) and tract (6).

My file is larger than the limit.

Split it into smaller files, or contact us to use the command-line version of this tool, which is built for large files.
